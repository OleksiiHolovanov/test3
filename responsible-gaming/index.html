Rabona responsible gaming: limits, warning signs and support

This page was rewritten to make safety tools more visible. It explains how Rabona account controls may help users set boundaries, recognise harmful patterns and pause activity when betting is no longer controlled entertainment.

Account tools to use before problems grow

Responsible gaming controls are placed first because they are practical actions, not secondary information. Configure them inside Settings → Responsible Gaming on the day you open the account.

💳

Deposit limits

Set daily, weekly or monthly caps. Reductions take effect immediately; increases require a 7-day cooling period.

⏱️

Session limits

Cap how long you stay logged in. The app will warn you 5 minutes before logging you out automatically.

🧊

Cooling-off

Lock the account for 24 hours, 7 days, 30 days or 90 days. The block is irreversible until the period ends.

🚫

Self-exclusion

A stronger block from 6 months up to permanent. Your data is retained only to enforce the exclusion.

📊

Activity history

Open Settings → History to see exact deposit, withdrawal and stake totals over any chosen period.

🔕

Marketing controls

Switch off bonus emails, push notifications and SMS if promotional triggers cause unplanned betting.

Self-assessment: nine honest questions

If you answer "yes" to three or more of the following, it is time to pause, lower limits and consider outside support.

Ask yourself

  • Have you bet more money than you could afford to lose in the last month?
  • Do you need to bet larger stakes to feel the same excitement?
  • Have you returned to bet again on another day to recover losses ("chasing")?
  • Have you borrowed money or sold anything to fund betting?
  • Have you felt guilty, anxious or depressed about your betting?
  • Has betting caused arguments at home or affected work or studies?
  • Have you hidden your account activity from people close to you?
  • Have you tried to cut down or stop and found you could not?
  • Do you bet when bored, lonely, stressed or after drinking?

Five practical strategies that actually work

Account tools are only part of the solution. These habits give the tools a chance to do their job.

  1. Set the budget before opening the appDecide the exact amount you can lose before you log in. Once that amount is gone, the session ends - no exceptions, no "one last bet".
  2. Use a separate payment method for bettingPre-load a single e-wallet or prepaid card with your monthly budget. When it is empty, your card cannot reach the cashier.
  3. Avoid betting under emotional stressTired, drunk, angry or grieving people make bets they would never place sober and rested. Pause for 24 hours before deciding.
  4. Never chase lossesDoubling the stake to recover a lost bet is the single fastest path to harm. Walk away from a losing session and come back another day with a fresh budget.
  5. Talk to someone you trustHiding losses makes them grow. Telling a partner, friend or family member breaks the secrecy that keeps the problem alive.

Myths vs reality

Myth: "I'm due a win after a losing streak."
Reality: Each bet is independent. Past results do not change future probability - this is the gambler's fallacy.

Myth: "Betting is a way to make extra income."
Reality: Sportsbooks build a margin (the "overround") into every market. Over time, that margin transfers money from bettors to the operator. Treat betting as paid entertainment, not as a side job.

Myth: "If I bet small enough, I cannot get into trouble."
Reality: Frequency causes harm as much as stake size. Hundreds of 1 USD live bets a day can damage finances and sleep just as severely as one large bet a week.

Myth: "I can stop whenever I want."
Reality: If stopping has been difficult more than once, the brain has already adapted. That is the moment to use self-exclusion or a blocker - not after the next loss.

Help for friends and family

If someone you love is showing signs of problem gambling, you do not have to handle it alone. Three steps that consistently help:

  • Protect shared finances first. Move savings and joint funds to an account they cannot access. Do not lend money or pay off gambling debts - this usually extends the problem.
  • Talk without judgement. Address the behaviour, not the person. Avoid ultimatums in the heat of an argument; agreements made calmly stick longer.
  • Get support for yourself. GamAnon and Gambling Therapy run dedicated groups for affected families. You are likely to need help too.

Protection of minors

Rabona access is restricted to users aged 18 or older. Anyone underage who creates an account will have it closed and any winnings voided. Keep login details private, log out fully on shared devices, use device-level restrictions and never allow minors to use an adult account.
